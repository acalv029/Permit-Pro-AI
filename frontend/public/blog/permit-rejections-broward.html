10 Reasons Your Permit Gets Rejected in Broward County (And How to Avoid Them)

These aren't guesses. They come straight from plan reviewer comment sheets, building department rejection lists, and hundreds of real permit submissions across 18 Broward cities.

By Alex Calvo · Updated June 2026 · 8 min read

If you've ever had a permit kicked back in Broward County, you know the drill. You drive to the building department, wait in line, hand over your package, and then get told you're missing something you didn't even know you needed. Now you're making another trip, your crew is sitting idle, and your client is asking why the project hasn't started yet.

The worst part is that most rejections are completely avoidable. They're not code violations or design problems. They're paperwork issues. Wrong form, missing signature, expired product approval, stuff that has nothing to do with whether the actual work is good.

Here are the 10 most common reasons permits get rejected in Broward County, based on actual plan reviewer feedback from cities including Oakland Park, Fort Lauderdale, Pompano Beach, and others.

#1. Plans Not Signed and Sealed by the Designer of Record

This is the number one rejection across every trade in every Broward city. Your plans need to be signed and sealed by a Florida-licensed architect or engineer. Not just stamped. Signed, sealed, and dated. Digital signatures are accepted on most portals now, but the seal has to meet the format requirements under Florida Statutes 471 and 481.

If you're submitting revised plans, they need to be signed by the original designer of record. You can't have one engineer draw the plans and a different one seal the revisions. Cities like Wilton Manors are especially strict on this.

#2. Missing or Incomplete Permit Application

You'd think this would be obvious, but incomplete applications are the second most common rejection reason. Every field needs to be filled out. Job address, folio number, scope of work, contractor info, owner info, job value. Leave one blank and it comes back.

Broward County updated the Uniform Building Permit Application in late 2025. If you're using an old version of the form, it gets rejected automatically. Print a fresh one from the Broward County website every time.

Wilton Manors requires all applications in black ink. Other ink colors get rejected on the spot.

#3. Expired Product Approvals (NOAs)

All of Broward County is in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ). That means every impact-rated product (windows, doors, shutters, roofing materials, garage doors) needs a valid Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA). The NOA has to be current at the time of permit issuance. If it expired even one day before your permit gets issued, the whole application gets rejected.

This one catches experienced contractors all the time. You spec a product, get the NOA, spend weeks assembling the rest of the package, and by the time you submit, the NOA has expired. Always check the expiration date right before you submit.

The NOA must cover your exact product configuration, not just the product line. A NOA for a 36x48 window doesn't cover a 36x60 of the same model.

#4. Missing Notice of Commencement (NOC)

Florida law requires a recorded Notice of Commencement for any project valued at $2,500 or more. It needs to be recorded with the Broward County Records Division before your first inspection. But many cities want it at permit application, not just before inspection.

The NOC has to list the correct contractor name, the correct property owner, and the correct property address. If the contractor on the NOC doesn't match the contractor on the application, it gets rejected.

Some cities have different NOC thresholds for HVAC work. Wilton Manors doesn't require a NOC for AC changeouts under $15,000. Dania Beach's threshold for HVAC is $7,500. Know your city.

#5. Wrong Edition of the Florida Building Code Referenced

The Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) took effect on January 1, 2024. If your plans reference the 7th Edition or earlier, they get rejected. This sounds simple but it trips up contractors who reuse plan templates from older projects. Your engineer needs to update the code reference on every new set of plans.

#6. Missing Energy Calculations

Energy calculations are required for nearly every permit type in Broward except simple repairs. New construction, additions, window replacements, HVAC changeouts, roofing — they all need energy calcs. For residential work, that's usually a Manual J load calculation and a Form 600A.

For HVAC changeouts specifically, you also need an AHRI certificate for the new unit. If your HVAC sub doesn't provide one, your mechanical permit gets rejected.

#7. Insurance Certificate Problems

Every city in Broward requires a Certificate of Liability Insurance. The certificate holder wording has to be exact. Not close. Exact. "City of Fort Lauderdale" is not the same as "Fort Lauderdale Building Department." Each city has its own required wording, and if your insurance company puts the wrong name on the cert, it gets kicked back.

#8. Missing Survey or Expired Survey

Most Broward cities require a current survey signed and sealed by a Florida-licensed surveyor. "Current" usually means within 1 to 2 years, depending on the city. Wilton Manors requires it within 2 years with the original seal. Some cities won't accept a copy, they want the original.

The survey needs to show actual lot dimensions, all building locations, easements, flood zone information, and base flood elevation. If any of that is missing, your application goes back.

#9. Contractor Name Mismatch

The contractor name on your permit application has to match the contractor name on your insurance certificate, your license, and your NOC. Exactly. If your license says "ABC Construction LLC" and your application says "ABC Construction," some plan reviewers will reject it.

This also applies when a licensed contractor pulls permits for another company. The application, insurance, license, and NOC all need to show the pulling contractor. The plans and drawings can show a different company, that's normal and legal.

#10. Digital Submission Problems

More Broward cities are going fully digital. Fort Lauderdale is 100% online through LauderBuild and no longer accepts paper applications at all. But digital submissions come with their own rejection reasons that didn't exist five years ago.

Common digital rejections include: files that can't be opened or are corrupted, PDFs that are copy-protected or secured, too many separate PDF files instead of one consolidated file, and missing digital signature verification. If you're submitting electronically, make sure your files are unlocked, properly named, and not split into 20 separate documents.

The Real Cost of a Rejection

A rejected permit isn't just an inconvenience. It's money. You're looking at another trip to the building department (gas, parking, time), another day or more of delay, a crew that's either sitting idle or getting sent to another job, and a client who's losing confidence. For a small contractor, one rejection can cost $200 to $500 in wasted time alone. An expediter charges $300 to $500 to prevent exactly this.

Most of these rejection reasons have nothing to do with the quality of your work. They're administrative. Paperwork. Forms. Signatures. Dates. The kind of stuff that a checklist catches in 30 seconds but costs you a full day when you miss it.
